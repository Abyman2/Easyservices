# EasyService: Addis Innovation Pitch Deck

## Slide 1 - Title

**EasyService**  
One trusted marketplace for discovering and booking Ethiopia's everyday services.

Team: Abel Seleshe, Baheran Tesfaye, Nebiyu Yohannes, Wondesen Teshale  
Addis Ababa University | September 2026

## Slide 2 - The problem

Customers currently search across informal channels, phone calls, social posts, and disconnected provider pages. This creates uncertainty about availability, identity, price, payment, and cancellation.

Providers face the opposite problem: valuable local inventory is difficult to present, schedule, and sell digitally.

## Slide 3 - The opportunity

Ethiopia has a large, mobile-first customer base and a diverse service economy spanning hospitality, transport, events, and local products. A trusted transaction layer can convert discovery into confirmed demand.

## Slide 4 - The solution

EasyService combines:

- Searchable local listings
- Verified customer and provider identities
- Inventory-aware booking
- Transparent simulated wallet payment
- Promotions and provider management
- Booking status, cancellation, and refund flows

## Slide 5 - Product demo journey

1. Customer opens the marketplace.
2. Customer selects a verified persona or logs in.
3. Customer filters and selects a listing.
4. Customer enters dates, quantity, and promotion.
5. The platform checks identity, availability, price, and balance.
6. The transaction is confirmed, inventory is reduced, and a receipt is shown.

## Slide 6 - Why this product is credible

The academic project is built as a testable product, not only a visual prototype:

- Spring Boot backend and Svelte frontend
- Unit, integration, and Selenium system tests
- Formal EP, BVA, decision-table, and state-transition design
- GitHub Actions and Jenkins pipeline definitions
- Defect log, metrics, and release decision

## Slide 7 - Differentiation

EasyService is designed around local trust signals and operational correctness. The same business rule controls the UI, API, inventory, payment simulation, and automated tests.

## Slide 8 - Business model

- Commission on completed bookings
- Provider subscription for enhanced visibility and analytics
- Sponsored listings with clear labeling
- Optional customer convenience fee for selected categories

The academic implementation uses in-memory data and simulated payment; production payments require regulated integrations.

## Slide 9 - Go-to-market

**Pilot:** Addis Ababa hospitality and event providers.  
**Expansion:** Car rental and curated local products.  
**Growth loop:** Provider supply improves customer choice; confirmed demand gives providers a reason to invite more supply.

## Slide 10 - Responsible scale

Before commercial launch, the product needs real payment compliance, privacy controls, production persistence, observability, dispute handling, provider verification operations, and stronger coverage evidence.

## Slide 11 - Financial snapshot

Illustrative base case from the feasibility model:

- Year 1 revenue: ETB 2.28M
- Year 2 revenue: ETB 7.20M
- Year 3 revenue: ETB 16.80M
- Illustrative break-even: month 18-20

These are planning assumptions, not audited forecasts.

## Slide 12 - The ask

Seeking a controlled pilot partnership, provider introductions, and seed support for production hardening, compliance, and customer discovery.

**Success metric for the pilot:** repeatable completed bookings with low cancellation and verified provider supply.

## Slide 13 - Closing

EasyService makes local services easier to find, safer to book, and more measurable to grow.
